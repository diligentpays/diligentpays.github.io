About

My path started with mathematics. I earned a B.S. from the University of Benin, Nigeria, where I graduated as the best student in the Department of Mathematics for the 2014/2015 session, receiving a distinction on my final year thesis on Fuzzy Groups. That foundation of formal rigor has shaped everything since.

I completed my M.S. in Computer Science (Data Science concentration) at Austin Peay State University (CGPA 3.91/4.00), where my capstone applied ensemble machine learning to auto insurance fraud detection. The most revealing result wasn't the accuracy score. It was a negative one: correcting the class imbalance barely moved performance, which told me the imbalance itself wasn't the main constraint. That habit of pushing past convenient results drives both my research and my engineering work.

By day I'm a Network Automation Engineer at GEICO, building the infrastructure automation and observability platforms that keep the core network running. On the side, I co-founded CareLink AI, a health-tech startup building a risk-stratification model for elderly patients, shaped by ongoing research and consultation with nurses and geriatricians, not just the data.

I am applying to PhD programs in Computer Science and Computational Sciences for Fall 2027, with a research focus on trustworthy AI, data security and privacy, and machine learning for healthcare and critical infrastructure.

Research Interests

My research question: what does trustworthiness require at every layer of a computational system: the network, the model, and the human acting on its output, and can any one layer be secured in isolation from the other two?

This question emerges from my own work: fraud-detection models bounded by infrastructure constraints, healthcare ML models whose value depends on clinician trust, and network automation systems operating across infrastructure I don't fully control. I want the rigor of doctoral research to answer it properly.
